# AeroSim — Demo Script (3–5 minutes)

Preparation: backend on :8000, frontend on :5173, browser on **Fleet overview**. One click on *Run demo sequence* runs everything; the rest of the script shows the live 3D twin, which is the part that makes the judges lean in.

## 0:00 — The problem (Fleet overview, hero)

"Aircraft components degrade by different mechanisms — fatigue and crack growth in an engine mount, creep in a turbine blade, seal wear in an actuator. Three kinds of information exist about a component: what its flights did to it, what the physics says that loading should do, and what an inspection actually found. AeroSim keeps an individual digital twin per physical component — this is engine mount ENG-MOUNT-001, serial SN-EM-0187, on aircraft DEMO-001; its right-hand sister part has its own separate twin — and learns flight by flight from the component's own history."

Point at the 3D aircraft: "Every marker is a live component twin, colour-coded by how close it is to its review limit — green, amber, red. This isn't a picture; it's driven by the same numbers you'll see update on the next screen." Click **animate flight** briefly to show the aircraft pitch through taxi → climb → cruise.

## 0:30 — Open the live twin (Live twin page)

Click a marker, then **Open live twin**. "This is the actual 3D model of the component — procedural geometry, not a stock asset — reacting in real time to the flight it's about to fly. Press **Fly**."

As the flight plays: "The part strains and heats with the forecast — load tints the beam, temperature glows the metal, vibration jitters the whole assembly. The crack site is labelled live: millimetres and percentage of critical length, both updating. On the right, four gauges show the pre-flight forecast band against the sensor trace as it's revealed — cyan is what we predicted before the flight, amber is what the sensors actually read, and the residual is computed live."

Let it run to the end (or scrub with the slider to skip ahead — ×120 speed covers a 2-hour flight in a minute). "At the end of the flight the evidence gate reveals its decision: forecast error splits into schedule deviation — not a model error — and response-model error, which is the only part allowed to correct the twin."

## 1:45 — Run the full demo sequence (back on Fleet overview)

Press **Run demo sequence**. While it runs (≈ 5 s): "Everything you've just seen and everything here is synthetic demonstration data generated by a hidden reference model; the twin never reads that model." Steps stream in one by one:

1–5. "Aircraft, component profile, its 12 historical flight observation records, its inspection findings — a 5.9 mm crack found in August, re-measured in October — and its maintenance disposition."
6. "The twin estimates the current condition by replaying its own history chronologically: before each historical flight it predicts, then reconciles against what was observed, and only inspections move the crack estimate. Model version 15; the load-transfer factor was learned as 1.118 — the hidden truth is 1.12."
7–8. "Next scheduled flight AC001-F0013: planned duration, payload and forecast are known; manoeuvre intensity and vibration are not — they come from comparable flights on the same route and are tagged as estimates. The prediction is stored immutably with its model and input-data version."
9–10. "The flight takes place. Forecast error 6 %: 3.6 % is schedule deviation (the flight lasted 122 minutes, not 120), 2.4 % is the response model — only that part may correct the model."
11. "Stage A: response parameters corrected, flight incorporated. The crack estimate did not move — sensor data cannot establish damage."
12–13. "A UT measurement arrives: 6.01 ± 0.1 mm. Stage B fuses it with the model estimate: 6.14 → 6.02 mm. The Paris coefficient is left unchanged because the growth since the last inspection is not identifiable against the measurement uncertainty."
14. "The next scheduled flight is predicted with the updated twin: remaining life 346 flights of the reference mission."
15. "The chronological experiment on the component's own 13 flights: static model, calibrated model, two-stage model, two-stage with sparse inspections — error measured against the hidden reference."

## 2:40 — Validation (open the Validation page)

Lead with the scoreboard: "Static model versus self-evolving twin, same component, same flights, measured against a hidden reference it never sees: load forecast error 18.8 % → 10.2 %; final crack error 5.9 mm → 0.37 mm; remaining-life estimate 23,116 flights → 323, against a true 298. Model B — operationally calibrated only — halves the forecast error but its crack error is unchanged, which is exactly the point: operational correction and damage updating are different problems, and conflating them is the mistake we're arguing against. 111 backend tests pass; nothing on this page is hard-coded."

## 3:40 — Manual loop and evidence gate (Reconciliation page)

"Any step can be done by hand, or replayed in the live twin — this button jumps straight there." Select the pending prediction for AC001-F0014, inject a data-link dropout, fly, compare: "44 % missing samples, no channel passes the reliability screen — the gate rejects the update and the model version does not change." On Inspections: "a 3 mm crack reading on a 6 mm crack is rejected as physically contradictory."

## 4:15 — Positioning and limits

"Nothing here is unprecedented individually; our proposed contribution is the controlled separation of operational correction from inspection-confirmed damage updating, with an evidence gate, measured chronologically against a hidden reference, and made legible through a live 3D twin instead of a table of numbers. Limits: synthetic data, illustrative material coefficients, uncalibrated uncertainty intervals, no airworthiness or dispatch decisions."

## How to pitch this to a judge in 30 seconds (if you're cut short)

"Most 'digital twin' demos are a dashboard. Ours is a physically modelled component — four different degradation mechanisms, four different 3D models — that you watch fly, strain, heat, and crack in real time, built on a backend that actually enforces the hard rule everyone in this space gets wrong: sensor data can correct how a part responds to load, but only an inspection can tell you it's damaged. We prove that rule matters with a controlled experiment against a hidden ground truth: conflating the two gets you 5.9 mm of crack-length error; keeping them separate gets you 0.37 mm. Click Fly and watch it happen."

## Anticipated questions

* **Why not update crack length from strain residuals?** A residual says the load model is off, not that the material changed; using it as damage would double-count and hide load-model errors. Only inspection evidence changes the state (Stage B), and the experiment shows why: B equals A on damage error.
* **How do you avoid using post-flight data in a pre-flight prediction?** Predictions store the input-data version; condition resolution only uses flights with an earlier event time; a prediction is refused for a completed flight; tests enforce it (`test_no_leakage_from_future_flights`, `test_prediction_is_immutable_and_pre_flight_only`).
* **Is the 3D model just a animation on top of fixed data, or is it really driven by the physics?** It reads the same `predicted_channels`, `state_before`/`state_after` and reconciliation records the backend already stores — the `/twin/{id}/playback/{flight_id}` endpoint derives a documented intra-flight trajectory (load-weighted or Arrhenius-weighted) from the single stored per-flight increment, but the increment, the forecast band and the sensor trace are the real immutable records, not invented for the viewer.
* **Why RLS and not a Kalman filter?** The response factor is a constant with a linear measurement model; scalar RLS is the simplest estimator that can be validated against batch least squares (test included).
* **Do two engine mounts of the same part number share a state?** No — separate instances, histories and hidden references; ENG-MOUNT-002 has no crack and its own calibrated k_L = 0.97.
* **What happens on a replacement?** The maintenance record resets the twin state and response priors; HYD-ACT-001's seal replacement is in the history and the second demo.
* **Are the intervals calibrated?** No; they are Monte Carlo sample percentiles under stated input distributions and the UI says so.
* **What would it take for operational use?** Qualified material data, flight-test-derived response models, calibrated sensor models, real inspection histories, independent verification of the updating logic, and certification considerations beyond this prototype.
